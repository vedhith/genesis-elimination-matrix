Unconception

/ˌʌnkənˈsɛpʃən/noun · proper noun when capitalised

un·con·cep·tion  ·  from un- (not) + conception (a concept held in the mind; also a beginning)

  1. What lies outside every concept.

    Not code, not a box, not any concept at all. Not nothing either. Something you cannot think of, so it has no true definition. It is not about whether we can know it, cannot know it, or might know it later: it is outside the full scope of human consciousness as it currently is, all of it together.

    “It isn't code, it isn't any concept, it isn't even a concept itself, just something else.” Turn 10.
  2. The beginning that cannot be thought.

    Conception is also a start. The Unconception is the origin that has no origin story a mind can hold: the genesis that cannot itself be conceived. In the chart, everything above us eventually ends here.

    “As you go above us everything eventually ends to unconcept.” Turn 14.
  3. A concept that does not exist, and that is it.

    The word names the fact that there is nothing to name. The concept itself is the thing: the unconcept.

    “A concept that doesn't exist but that is it: the concept itself, the unconcept.”

The concept, plainly

Take everything a human mind can hold: every idea, every picture, every equation, every religion, every “nothing”. Draw a line around all of it. The Unconception is what is on the other side of that line. Not a bigger idea. Not a hidden idea. The place where ideas stop.

That is why nothing can be said about it, including that it is empty, or good, or one, or many. Saying any of that would be holding it in a concept, and then it would not be the Unconception. The only honest sentences are the ones that say what it is not.

It is also why the chart has a top. Every step up makes things simpler and more whole, like going from code to folder. Follow that all the way and you run out of concepts. What is left is this.
