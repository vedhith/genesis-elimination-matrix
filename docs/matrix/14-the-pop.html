14 — The Pop Hypothesis

Owner's statement: we went from single cell to animal to consciousness. Each step made the previous step understandable and made the next look impossible. Thinking, time, and the rest may be concepts that are simple once crossed. Each crossing is a squeeze, the squeeze gets harder, then a pop, and everything before makes sense.

Where it sits

It is not a fifth pillar. It is a claim about the shape of the fog: that every barrier we meet is F3 (closed to us from here) and none is F4 (closed to all). In matrix terms: F4 is empty, or at least every wall met so far was fog. It is the strongest possible version of the moving-fog theorem.

Prior art

  • Major evolutionary transitions (Maynard Smith and Szathmáry 1995): eight recognized jumps, each a new level of organization.
  • Anderson, "More Is Different" (1972): each level has laws not deducible in practice from below, and each level explains the one below.
  • Teilhard's Omega Point; Hegel's dialectic; Kurzweil's singularity; Vinge (1993), who called the singularity "a wall we cannot see past," which is the fog front in the owner's sense.
  • Phase transitions and criticality for "squeeze then pop."
  • Modis (2002) and Kurzweil: canonical milestones spaced roughly geometrically in time, which is the "squeezing harder."

The math, honestly

Track record. Take the eight major transitions as trials. All eight were crossed by our lineage. Laplace's rule of succession gives the chance that the next barrier is also crossable:

P(next crossable) = (s + 1) / (n + 2) = 9 / 10 = 0.90

Survivorship correction. We can only observe from inside a lineage that crossed every barrier. Lineages stopped by a wall are not here to report. So the eight successes are partly selected, not sampled. There is no clean correction; a reasonable discount puts the figure at roughly 0.7 to 0.8.

The squeeze. Approximate dates of the jumps, in years before now:

Jump Years ago
Life 3.8 × 10⁹
Eukaryotic cell 2 × 10⁹
Multicellular life 6 × 10⁸
Nervous systems 5 × 10⁸
Language 2 × 10⁵
Writing 5 × 10³
Science 5 × 10²
Computing 7 × 10¹

Successive gaps shrink by roughly a factor of 2 to 40. The spacing is close to geometric on a log scale. That is the owner's "squeezing harder," and it is visible in the data, with the caveat that the choice of milestones is subjective and critics have shown different lists give different curves.

What the induction cannot do. It says the next wall is probably fog. It cannot say there is no final wall. Each pop pushes the estimate forward by one step; it never reaches the limit. So:

P(next barrier is fog, not wall)      ≈ 0.7 – 0.9   (estimable)
P(no final wall anywhere: F4 empty)   not estimable from inside

This is exactly the owner's own Turn 14 point: waking up moves the fog, and you can never check whether it moves forever.

What it predicts

  1. The hard problem of consciousness is a squeeze, not a wall. After the pop, experience will look as simple as cells look now.
  2. Time will look like a concept, not a container, from the next level. Some physics already leans this way (emergent spacetime).
  3. Each level's theories become effective theories of the level below, cleaner and shorter. Already true for chemistry over physics, biology over chemistry.

What would count against it

  • A barrier that stays put while everything around it advances for a very long time. The hard problem has held for about thirty years in its current form and about three centuries since Leibniz's mill. Not yet long enough.
  • A proof, in the Gödel style, that a specific question about our own level is undecidable from any level built from ours. FG already exists inside mathematics. An FG result about consciousness would be the first true wall.

Verdict

The tree does not prove it. The tree is compatible with it and the record favors it for the next step, at roughly three chances in four. Whether the pops go on forever, or end at the Akatalepton, is the one thing the pattern cannot tell you, and the owner already found the reason why.
