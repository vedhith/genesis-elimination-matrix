07 — The Fog Front: What Can an Observer at a Given Position Know?

This document owns axis F (knowability) and gives the owner's Fog Front a precise form that survives the owner's own Turn 14 objection.

1. Definitions

  • Position p: an observer's total epistemic situation: sense organs, instruments, formal systems available, and the layer of reality it inhabits.
  • K(p): the set of claims an observer at p can settle (prove, disprove, or show undecidable) in principle, given unlimited time at that position.
  • K_now(p): the subset settleable with instruments actually built.
  • Fog Front at p: the boundary of K(p). Everything outside it is fog from p.

2. Axis F — knowability status of a claim, relative to p_now

Code Value Meaning Examples at To Nyn
F0 Settled Already tested, result in hand. fMRI decodes gist, not verbatim; glia ≈ neurons; no Planck-scale pixelation found at current sensitivity.
F1 Testable with foreseeable technology Inside K(p_now), not yet in K_now. Which correlates track reported experience (GWT vs IIT); whether whole-brain emulation reproduces behavior; lattice gauge theory on quantum computers.
F2 Knowable in principle, no known test Inside K(p_now) but no experiment designed. Whether spacetime is emergent from entanglement in our (de Sitter) universe; whether there is a Layer 2 author.
FG Provably undecidable within our formal systems Inside our logic, outside K(p) for any p using the same logic. Gödel sentences; the halting problem; Chaitin's Ω digits beyond a point.
F3 Closed to our position, open to some possible position B2a. Whether other systems have experience (Axiom 6), if a mind with direct access is coherent; the nature of Layer 3 and above.
F4 Closed to every position B2b. The Un-Concept proper. By definition, nothing can be listed here except the label.

Exhaustiveness: settled or not; if not, testable soon, testable eventually, provably undecidable, closed to us but not all, closed to all. Complete.

3. The two components of the Fog Front

Proven core (FG). Gödel (1931): any consistent formal system rich enough to express arithmetic contains true statements it cannot prove. Turing (1936): no general procedure decides whether a program halts. Chaitin: most digits of the halting probability are unknowable. These are inside our logic, are not in dispute, and are already a Fog Front. The owner's intuition that "a system cannot read its own code" is partly vindicated here, though the correct statement is "a system cannot prove all truths about itself," which is weaker.

Speculative extension (F3, F4). The claim that metaphysical questions (axes A, B, C, D) lie beyond K(p) for every human p. This is McGinn's cognitive closure applied broadly. It is a hypothesis. Evidence for it is the persistence of the hard problem; evidence against it is that many questions once called permanently mysterious (the composition of stars, the origin of species) moved from F3 to F0.

4. The moving-fog theorem (the owner's Turn 14, formalized)

Claim: For positions p₁ below p₂ (where p₂ has strictly more instruments or a higher layer), K(p₁) ⊆ K(p₂), and typically the inclusion is strict. Therefore a claim in F3 at p₁ may be in F1 or F0 at p₂.

Corollary 1: "X is unknowable" without a position index is not well-formed. The owner's "we can prove the fog front is real no matter what" is true only in the position-relative form: for every p, the boundary of K(p) is non-empty (guaranteed by FG alone).

Corollary 2: The absolute form, there exists X outside K(p) for all p, is exactly the claim that F4 is non-empty, which is the claim that the Un-Concept exists. It cannot be proven from inside any p, because proving it would require surveying all positions. It can only be a posit.

Corollary 3 (the owner's self-refutation, resolved): "Maybe we wake up from the Un-Concept to us right now and know more" describes moving from p₁ to p₂. It shows that what looked like F4 from p₁ was F3. It does not show F4 is empty. So the owner was right that the fog moves, and right that this undermines proving the Un-Concept; but the position-relative Fog Front survives untouched.

5. What is stable across all positions

Only these:

  1. FG is non-empty at every position that uses a consistent formal system capable of arithmetic.
  2. Axiom 6 (behavior is not experience) holds at every position that observes other systems from outside.
  3. The boundary of K(p) exists for every p.

Everything else about the fog is position-indexed.

7. Content fog vs nature fog (from 04-mind.md)

Two things were called "fog" in the conversation and must be kept apart:

Kind Claim F status
Content fog Decoders can only read active working memory; a person can resist. F0, and shallow: better instruments reduce it.
Nature fog No third-person method settles whether a system experiences. F3 at least; possibly F4 if "direct access to another mind" is incoherent.

8. Does moving the fog have a cost?

The owner suggested (Turn 15) that tools like libraries and Google expand K_now while shrinking individual capability. In matrix terms: K_now(p) for a civilization can grow while K_now(individual) shrinks, because the civilization's position includes the tools and the individual's may not. This is a real distinction (distributed cognition; Clark and Chalmers 1998). The empirical claim that it makes individuals "dumber" is weak (Flynn effect; mixed replications of the Google-effect study). The structural point stands: p can be a person, a lab, or a species, and the fog is different for each.
