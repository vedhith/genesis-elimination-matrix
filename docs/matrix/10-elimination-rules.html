10 — Elimination Rules: How to Play the Game Without Cheating

The owner's stated goal (Turn 12): "if we can test this chart we can then pinpoint if it's unknowable or knowable by knocking out the knowable theories ... that's the game." These rules make the game valid. Every rule closes a hole that the informal version had.

Rule 1 — Eliminate cells, not names

A named theory may be eliminated only by eliminating a cell it occupies. If the theory can relocate to a neighboring cell without losing its content, it is not eliminated; it is moved. Record the move.

Why: named theories are elastic. "Simulation hypothesis" survives any physics result by adjusting the simulator's capabilities. Only the cell D1a ∧ (specific rendering artifact predicted) is eliminable.

Rule 2 — Elimination requires an exhaustive partition

You may conclude "therefore the remaining cell is true" only if the cells form a partition proven exhaustive by Axiom 1 (excluded middle or a proven finite enumeration). Over a list of names, the conclusion is invalid.

Why: the four-pillar version eliminated Code, Sandbox and Clutter and concluded Un-Concept. It missed C1 D2, C4, C5 and A3 entirely (02-genesis.md §6). Over the partition, the same three eliminations leave five live regions, not one.

Rule 3 — Unexplained is not inexplicable (Axiom 5)

A cell is eliminated by a positive result it forbids, or by a proof of internal inconsistency. A cell is never eliminated by the absence of a successful theory within it.

Why: the informal game said "if none of the knowable theories explain consciousness, the Un-Concept is proven." That infers F4 from F1/F2 failure. Corollary 2 in 07-fog-front.md shows F4 cannot be proven from any position. Persistent failure raises the credence of F3/F4 and nothing more.

Rule 4 — Index every knowability claim to a position (Axiom 2)

"Unknowable" must read "outside K(p)" with p named. When To Nyn moves, re-check. A claim in F3 at one To Nyn may be F1 at the next.

Rule 5 — Distinguish improbable from impossible

Use the five-level scale from 06-morality.md §6:

Level Label Eliminates?
0 Consistent, live No
1 Under evidential pressure No (lowers credence)
2 Needs an added premise No (records the premise)
3 Category error No (reclassifies to another cell)
4 Formally inconsistent Yes

Only level 4 eliminates. Gate 1 (problem of evil) reaches level 4 only when "gratuitous" is held fixed, which theists contest; otherwise it is level 1. Gate 2 is level 3: it relocates, it does not eliminate.

Rule 6 — One test, one axis

Record for each test which single axis value it bears on and in which direction. A test that "supports simulation" must name the cell (D1a with which sub-property) and the alternative it disfavors. See 09-tests.md columns.

Rule 7 — Behavior tests never reach experience (Axiom 6)

No result of T05, T06, T07 or any emulation, biocomputing or AI benchmark may be recorded as bearing on axis C's experiential content. They may bear on C2-f vs C2-b functional predictions only.

Rule 8 — Author claims never reach ground claims

A result about Layer 2 (D1a author: exists, has stance S) says nothing about axes A and B. Do not propagate upward. The owner's "just another box" is this rule.

Rule 9 — Value never descends without a bridge (Axiom 7)

No genesis result eliminates or establishes an M or N cell without a stated bridging premise. The constraint table in 06-morality.md §4 lists the only propagations currently justified.

Rule 10 — Record eliminations in the tests registry, dated

An elimination is recorded as: cell, test ID, date, result, and the rule under which it counts. Undated eliminations are void.

Rule 11 — Do not let evaluators score the game (Axiom 9)

Praise from an AI system or any single evaluator does not change a cell's status. Only registry entries do.

Rule 12 — Preserve the eliminated

Eliminated cells stay on the map, marked. History of the fog is data (Axiom 10).
