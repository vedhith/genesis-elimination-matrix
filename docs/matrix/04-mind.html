04 — Mind: What Is Consciousness and What Are We?

Axis C applied to the observer. The question that started the whole conversation ("are we more than just neurons?") lives here. This document partitions theories of consciousness and records what each one says we are.

1. The hard problem, stated precisely

Easy problems: explain discrimination, integration, reportability, attention, control. These are functional and are being solved.

Hard problem (Chalmers 1995): explain why any of that functioning is accompanied by experience at all. Every position below is a stance on whether the hard problem is (a) real and solvable, (b) real and unsolvable, or (c) not real.

2. Axis C applied: the partition of consciousness theories

Code Family Claim about experience Claim about what we are Hard problem stance Named theories
C2-i Illusionism / eliminativism There is no experience as introspection presents it; the appearance of it is a cognitive illusion. Physical systems that misrepresent their own processing. (c) not real Dennett; Frankish; Graziano's attention schema theory (partly).
C2-f Functionalism / computationalism Experience is a functional or computational property; anything with the right organization has it. Patterns of information processing, substrate-independent. (a) real, solvable Global Workspace Theory (Baars, Dehaene); Higher-Order Thought (Rosenthal); Attention Schema (Graziano); predictive processing accounts (Seth, Clark); the owner's "we are the information the neuron holds."
C2-b Biological naturalism Experience is a property of specific physical (biological or causal-structural) organization, not of computation as such. Particular physical systems with the right causal structure. (a) real, solvable Integrated Information Theory (Tononi, Koch); Searle's biological naturalism; Block's biological view.
C1 Panpsychism / idealism Experience is fundamental; brains organize or filter it. Local concentrations, or dissociated parts, of a fundamental experiential field. (a) dissolved by making experience basic; combination problem replaces it Constitutive panpsychism (Goff, Strawson); cosmopsychism; analytic idealism (Kastrup); Russellian monism (C4-leaning).
C4 Neutral / dual-aspect monism Experience and physics are two aspects of one neutral base. Aspects of the neutral base. (a) partially dissolved Russell; Spinoza; Chalmers's "Type-F."
C5 Dualism Experience is a distinct substance or a distinct fundamental property. Minds attached to bodies, or bodies with extra properties. (a) real, answered by positing a second kind Descartes; Chalmers's naturalistic property dualism; Samkhya.
B2a-m Mysterianism Experience has a physical explanation but human minds cannot grasp it. Physical systems cognitively closed to their own nature. (b) real, unsolvable for us McGinn; Pinker (sympathetic); Chomsky.

Exhaustiveness: the hard problem is not real (C2-i), or it is real and experience is derivative (C2-f, C2-b), or it is real and experience is fundamental (C1, C4, C5), or it is real and closed to us (B2a-m). Complete.

3. What the Gemini conversation got right and wrong here

  • "Absolute proof the mind is tightly bound to neurons" — correct: lesion, stimulation, and pharmacology data establish dependence. Every family above accepts this. Dependence does not settle identity.
  • "Zero proof we are just neurons" — correct as stated, but every family above also has zero proof. The evidence underdetermines the C value.
  • "Consciousness is an emergent property" — this is one family (C2-f or C2-b), presented as if it were the consensus. There is no consensus.
  • "Glia outnumber neurons" — wrong (ratio about 1:1). Irrelevant to the C value either way.

4. Where the owner's own positions land

Owner's statement Cell
"we are the information the neuron holds" (Turn 6) C2-f (or C3 substrate)
"what if feeling is the very thing from the start" (Turn 7) C1
"the hard problem is the root and we can never know it" (Turn 7) B2a-m or B2b
"we are more than neurons" (Turn 5, conclusion) anything except C2-i and strict C2-b identity

The owner moved between C2-f, C1 and B2a-m within three turns. That is not a flaw; it shows the evidence does not force a choice. The matrix's job is to keep the options separate so that a later test can bear on one without the others.

5. Tests and their reach (see 09-tests.md for the registry)

Test What it can settle What it cannot settle (Axiom 6)
Cogitate adversarial collaboration (GWT vs IIT, results 2023–2025) Which functional or structural correlates track reported experience. Both theories lost specific predictions. Whether the correlates are experience.
Perturbational Complexity Index (Casali 2013) Clinical detection of consciousness in unresponsive patients. Nothing about C.
Whole-brain emulation Whether behavior can be reproduced from connectome plus dynamics. Whether the emulation experiences anything.
Biocomputing (Cortical Labs, organoids) Learning efficiency of neural tissue; whether neuron-like dynamics matter for intelligence. Whether the tissue experiences anything. Owner's question "would biocomputing find this" — no.
Quantum computing Nothing on this axis directly. Could test Penrose–Hameroff Orch-OR if a specific quantum-coherence prediction is made. Everything else.
Brain–computer interfaces and neural bridging Bandwidth, plasticity, whether a brain can incorporate external memory. Whether the merged system's experience changes.
Decoding (fMRI, MEG) Which neural states carry which contents. Nothing about C.

The one route that could move the C axis is a positive result predicted by one family and forbidden by others, for instance a measurable physical effect of mental causation (favors interactionist C5) or a derivation of experiential reports from physics that leaves nothing unexplained (favors C2). Neither is in hand at To Nyn.

6. The self-control thread (Turns 3 and 4)

"Are we more than neurons, so we can control it?" Control does not require any particular C value. Under C2, control is the prefrontal network modulating lower circuits; under C1, it is mind organizing itself; under C5, it is mind acting on matter. The criticality literature (brain near a phase transition, Beggs and Plenz 2003) is a C-neutral dynamical claim. Nothing in the control question discriminates between cells. It was a good question that happened to have the same answer in every cell: yes, the system regulates itself.

7. Mind and the Fog Front

Two distinct fog claims are often run together:

  1. Content fog: decoders can only read what is currently active in working memory. True, well-established, and uninteresting for the matrix.
  2. Nature fog: no observer can settle from the outside whether a system experiences. This is Axiom 6 and is the deepest fog inside our logic that is not merely technological. It is a candidate for B2a status: a differently built mind might have direct access to other minds. Whether that is even coherent is open.
