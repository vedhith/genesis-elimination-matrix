06 — Morality: Are There Moral Facts, and Which Survive Each Genesis Cell?

This document owns axes M (meta-ethics) and N (normative ethics), and does the work Axiom 7 demands: it shows how, if at all, a genesis cell constrains what anyone ought to do. The owner asked for a map that "intercaps every possible theory morality everything." This is the morality half.

1. Axis M — Are there moral facts?

Either there are moral facts whose truth does not depend on anyone's attitudes, or there are not.

Code Value Meaning Example positions
M1a Realism, ground-based Moral facts are grounded in the nature or will of the terminal ground. Divine command theory; natural law (Aquinas); Platonic Form of the Good; Dharma as cosmic order.
M1b Realism, naturalist Moral facts are natural facts (about welfare, flourishing, cooperation), discoverable empirically. Cornell realism (Boyd, Sturgeon); Railton; Aristotle on one reading; Harris's Moral Landscape.
M1c Realism, non-naturalist Moral facts are real, mind-independent, but not natural or supernatural; known by intuition or reason. Moore; Parfit (On What Matters); Enoch; Huemer.
M2a Anti-realism, error theory Moral claims aim at facts and all are false. Mackie (1977); Joyce.
M2b Anti-realism, expressivism Moral claims do not aim at facts; they express attitudes. Ayer; Blackburn; Gibbard.
M2c Anti-realism, constructivism Moral facts exist but are constructed by agents' rational or contractual activity. Kant (on one reading); Rawls; Korsgaard; Scanlon.
M2d Anti-realism, relativism Moral truth is relative to a culture or framework. Harman; Wong; folk relativism.

Exhaustiveness: realism or not. Realism splits by what grounds the facts (ground, nature, neither). Anti-realism splits by what moral talk is doing (failing, expressing, constructing, indexing). Complete for known positions; a new anti-realist account would be M2e.

2. Axis N — Given moral facts or constructions, what makes acts right?

Code Value Criterion Named theories
N1 Consequentialist Outcomes. Utilitarianism (Bentham, Mill, Singer); effective altruism; negative utilitarianism.
N2 Deontological Duties, rules, rights. Kant; Ross; rights theories (Nozick).
N3 Virtue Character and flourishing. Aristotle; Confucius; MacIntyre; Stoicism.
N4 Contractarian What rational agents would agree to. Hobbes; Rawls; Gauthier; Scanlon.
N5 Care and particularist Relationships and situated response. Noddings; Held; Dancy.
N6 Divine command The will of the author or ground. Ockham; Adams; Ash'arite theology.

N is not a strict partition (theories mix), so a position may carry braces, N{1,3}. It is included because the owner asked for "morality everything," and because the S axis (author's stance) bears directly on N6.

3. The two classic obstacles (Axiom 7)

Hume's is–ought gap. No set of purely descriptive premises entails a normative conclusion. Every arrow from a genesis cell to an M or N cell needs a bridging premise. The table in §4 names the bridge each time.

The Euthyphro dilemma. Does the author command X because X is good, or is X good because the author commands it? If the first, goodness is independent of the author (M1b or M1c, not M1a). If the second, goodness is arbitrary and "the author is good" is empty. This dilemma cuts M1a in half: M1a survives only with a specific answer (Aquinas: God's nature, not God's will, is the standard; Adams: God's commands plus God's loving nature).

Reading the table: M1b, M1c and M2 survive every cell. M1a survives only in cells with a graspable, willing ground, and then faces Euthyphro and Gate 1. That is the single most useful result of the morality map: the genesis question does much less work for ethics than intuition suggests. Whatever the origin, the live options are the same three families, and the arguments for and against them are internal to ethics.

5. Gate 1 restated for morality

The problem of evil (see 05-authorship.md §4) is a constraint on D1 ∧ S+, not on morality directly. Its moral relevance: if it succeeds, M1a loses its most popular home cell and must relocate to non-theistic grounds (Platonic C3, or a valenced C1 ground) or dissolve into M1b/M1c. The owner's argument in Turn 13 was aimed at religions; its real target is the M1a–D1b link.

Nothing on this scale is "hysterical." The owner's word was a search for "apophatic" (Gemini's identification) or possibly "heretical." Both are recorded in the glossary.
