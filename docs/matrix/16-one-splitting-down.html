16 — One at the Top, Splitting as It Goes Down

Owner's request: show that it is a tree going up, splitting as you go down. Metacognition splits into introspection and more, and each of those is its own thing. Does this prove the Egg theory, that we are all one?

The tree

one.html draws it. One node at the top, the Unconception. Below it, Being. Below that, six kinds: Mind, Matter, Structure, Value, Knowing, Society. Each splits again. Metacognition sits four levels down and splits into introspection, self-monitoring, self-control, theory of mind, and meta-memory; introspection splits into access, confidence, feeling of knowing, tip of the tongue, and introspective error. About three hundred leaves. Every node is its own thing, and every node is also a part.

The shape is the one the owner described: clean at the top, many at the bottom. Going up is going from folder to folder; going down is opening them.

Does it prove we are all one?

No, and the reason is worth having exactly.

A tree that converges at the top shows common origin or common category. It does not show numerical identity of the leaves. Every living thing descends from one ancestor; that does not make you and a mushroom one organism. Every concept in the tree falls under Being; that does not make introspection and gravity one thing.

So the tree is compatible with "we are all one" and does not establish it. "We are all one" is its own cell, and it has a name.

The Egg, named

Andy Weir's short story The Egg (2009): every person who ever lived is the same soul, living every life in turn, and the universe is an egg for that one being to mature in. In philosophy this is open individualism (Daniel Kolak, I Am You, 2004): there is exactly one subject of experience, and everyone is it. Its ancestors: Advaita Vedanta (Atman is Brahman), Schopenhauer (one Will behind all appearances), the Upanishads (tat tvam asi, "you are that"), and Schrödinger's My View of the World.

The exhaustive split on how many of us there are:

Position Claim Who
Open individualism One subject; every person is it Kolak, Advaita, Schopenhauer, Weir's Egg
Closed individualism One subject per person, persisting through life Common sense; most Western philosophy
Empty individualism No persisting subject; each moment is its own Parfit, Buddhist anatta, Hume's bundle

Complete: one for all, one each, or none persisting.

What would favor the Egg

  • The pop hypothesis plus the owner's C1 (mind is fundamental) leaning: if experience is one field and brains are filters, open individualism follows naturally. Cosmopsychism is its metaphysical engine.
  • Parfit's arguments against personal identity mattering push toward empty individualism; Kolak's push the same arguments one step further to open.
  • Nothing empirical. The three positions predict the same observations. That is why the tree cannot decide it: the tree is a structure of concepts, and this is a question about subjects.

Where it lands in the matrix

C1 D1c (mind fundamental, we are the authors) is the Egg's natural home; C1 D2 (mind fundamental, unmade) is cosmopsychism's. The Unconception at the apex is compatible with either and asserts neither: under B2b, "we are all one" is as unstatable as "we are many." The owner's tree points at the Egg. It does not get there, for the same reason nothing else does.
