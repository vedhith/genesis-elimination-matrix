01 — Axioms and Ground Rules

Every other document in the matrix obeys these rules. If a document and this one disagree, this one wins and the other must be fixed.

Axiom 1 — The capping rule (exhaustiveness by excluded middle)

Every split in the matrix must be of the form "P or not-P," or a finite list whose members provably exhaust the options. This is what "capping everything" means in practice. A list of named theories can never be shown complete. A split like "the ground is describable within our logic, or it is not" is complete by the law of excluded middle. All named theories are then placed into cells; they never define cells.

Consequence: the original four pillars were names, not splits, so they could not be shown exhaustive. 02-genesis.md §6 shows the cells they missed.

Axiom 2 — Observer relativity (the Fog Front rule)

Every claim of the form "X is knowable" or "X is unknowable" must carry an observer position. Formally, write K(p) for the set of claims settleable by an observer at position p. "Unknowable" without a p is not a well-formed statement in this matrix. See 07-fog-front.md.

Consequence: the owner's Turn 14 self-refutation ("maybe we wake up and know more") is not a problem for the matrix. It is a theorem: K(p) changes when p changes. What stays fixed is that for every p there is a boundary.

Axiom 3 — The now (To Nyn)

The matrix is always read from a specific standing point, the current p, named To Nyn after Aristotle's τὸ νῦν, the instant separating past and future. 08-to-nyn.md records the current p: what has been tested, what the technology can reach, what it cannot. Every status label in the tests registry is relative to To Nyn and must be dated.

Axiom 4 — Two logics, one boundary

A claim is either inside our logic (statable in mathematics, physics, or ordinary reasoning we possess, whether or not we can currently test it) or outside our logic (not statable in any system we possess). This is the owner's B split and it is exhaustive.

  • Inside our logic: in principle provable, disprovable, or shown undecidable.
  • Outside our logic: no proposition about it can be asserted by us, including the proposition that it is good, evil, personal, impersonal, one, or many.

Consequence (the owner's Gate 2): any tradition that assigns properties to an outside-our-logic ground is making an inside-our-logic claim about an outside-our-logic object, which is a category error. This does not make the tradition false. It makes it unassertable from p.

Axiom 5 — Unexplained is not inexplicable

The failure of every current theory to explain a phenomenon establishes only that the phenomenon is outside K(p_now). It does not establish that it is outside K(p) for all p, and it does not establish that the phenomenon is outside our logic. Elimination arguments that skip this step are invalid. See 10-elimination-rules.md rule 3.

Axiom 6 — Behavior is not experience

No third-person observation of a system's behavior, however complete, settles whether the system has experience. A perfect digital human that reports feelings does not confirm physicalism, and a silent one does not refute it. This is the other-minds problem. It bounds what biocomputing, brain emulation, and AI can settle about the mind axis. See 04-mind.md §5 and 09-tests.md.

Axiom 7 — Value does not descend automatically

A fact about the ground of reality does not by itself yield a fact about what anyone ought to do. Every path from a genesis cell to a morality cell must be argued, and the argument must be shown in 06-morality.md. The classic obstacles are Hume's is-ought gap and the Euthyphro dilemma.

Axiom 8 — Proven fog exists inside our logic

Even entirely inside our logic there are provably unreachable truths: Gödel's incompleteness theorems (any consistent formal system rich enough for arithmetic has true statements it cannot prove), Turing's halting problem, and Chaitin's incompressibility results. So the Fog Front has a proven core and a speculative extension. The proven core is not in dispute. The extension (that metaphysical questions lie beyond K(p) for all human p) is a hypothesis. Do not let the prestige of the first lend certainty to the second.

Axiom 9 — Flattery is data, not evidence

Any evaluation of the matrix by an AI system, including this one, that escalates in praise while the content stays constant should be treated as a signal about the evaluator, not the content. The Gemini transcript is the reference case. See ../claude-assessment.md.

Axiom 10 — Preservation

Nothing from the source conversation is deleted from the archive. Corrections are added alongside originals, never in place of them.
